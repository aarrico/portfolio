## Biograpghy

I grew up in Steubenville, Ohio, about 50 miles west of Pittsburgh, PA.
I subsquently went to Duquesne University in Pittsburgh where I started
out as a physics & math major with the intention of becoming a teacher. I 
had to take a C++ course the first semester and absolutely fell in love.
This was my first real exposure to programming, outside of learning HTML for 
MySpace backgrounds like any good millenial. I was always interested in tech
from a young age. My grandfather always had the latest and greatest Gateway PC.
I still vividly remember watching him install and play Myst for the first time.
He told me once I learned to read he would teach me how to use the computer.
I was reading before I turned 4. That enthusiasm was still strong in college 
because I ended up triple majoring, bolting a computer science degree on top
of the phyiscs and math ones. 

I was still very interested in physics though. I did some research work the
summer before my senior year at Duquesne at the Pittsburgh Supercomputing Center
where I was looking into how to use CUDA, which was brand new, to perform 
Fast Fourier Transforms. This lead to my reseach advisor recommending I go
to University of Tennessee - Knoxville in the Materials Science & 
Engineering grad program, to work with one of his collegues who wanted someone 
with strong computing and physics background. I spent 2 years working on some
of the best supercomputers in the country to run molecular dynamics simulations
to study the thermodynamics of alloys. During my time there I realized I
enjoyed the computational work I was doing more than the science, so I 
decided to stop at a Master's degree and pursure software engineering as
a career.

I found my first software engineering job at Optricity, where our flagship 
product was a Java desktop application to help companies re-organize 
their distribution centers and warehouses based on rules and constraints
they set up. For example, in a grocery DC, you don't want hazardous
chemicals sitting above food product. I was also their first in-house 
engineer. Over 5 and a half years, I grew to become Director of Technology
and built their first engineering team. Since I was their first engineer
and coming from a scientific computing background, I had to learn how 
to be a true software engineer on my own. Despite the company being less
than 20 people total, we were a household name in the warehousing industry.

At some point during my tenure at Optricity, I decided I wanted to learn
from other experienced engineers to further my technical expertise.
I wound up at BlueMark, a company that makes "swag" material for companies
like DoorDash and Uber. I was only there for 11 months before they decided
to pivot and laid off the entire engineering department (along with most 
of the company). Some co-workers from BlueMark that had left before the 
layoff were at HourWork where they pulled me in to help lead the complete
rebuild of their product from a Java Spring Boot & no-code frontend to a modern
architecture. Unfortunately, HourWork was not immune to the downturn of 
the industry starting in 2023 and I lost my position there, but not 
before successfully launching the rebuild and migrating the users over!
